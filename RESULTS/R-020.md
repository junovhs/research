## Decision gate

**Question.** R-014's certificate bounds an exact *discrete* Euler recurrence. Its live-state falsifier asks whether the same omission admission could certify a *continuous* output. The first sub-question is cheap and decisive: how large is the time-discretization error of the Euler recurrence relative to the continuous ODE, and how does it scale with step size?

**Target and accuracy layer.** Terminal state of the periodic ODE on D=16 sites, observed in max-norm, against a continuous reference. Accuracy layer: **time discretization only**; spatial discretization is the exact ODE's own spatial grid (no PDE error budget), and no model error is considered.

**Equations.** For i=0..15 (periodic), u_i' = d(u_{i+1} − 2u_i + u_{i−1}) + u_i − u_i³ + A·s(t)·cos(2πi/D), with d=1, A=0.5, T=8, s(t) piecewise linear through knots (0,0), (2,1), (4,−0.5), (6,0.75), (8,0). Initial state u0 is a fixed 16-vector in [−0.9, 0.9].

**Euler recurrence.** u^{n+1} = u^n + h·F(t_n, u^n), h = 1/m, m ∈ {16,…,1024}, steps = T/h.

**Reference.** SciPy DOP853, rtol = atol = 1e-12, max_step 0.125, split at forcing knots.

**Strongest alternatives.** Higher-order explicit Runge–Kutta, Taylor/interval-validated integrators (e.g. VNODE-type), and a Richardson-extrapolated Euler. Only the first-order Euler is compared here, since it is R-014's actual recurrence.

**Decision rule fixed before the run.** If the error falls only linearly and a 1e-9 target needs steps comparable to the omitted-history workload's whole-history cost, the continuous extension of the R-014 certificate is uneconomic. This is an analytic extrapolation of measured data, not a certified bound.

## Consequences for the R-014 line

- To make the Euler-based terminal output match the continuous ODE to 1e-9 (in max-norm, this D=16 setting), the step needed is h ≈ 1e-9 / 0.1416 ≈ 7.1e-9, i.e. roughly **1.1×10⁹ steps** over T=8. R-014's million-step cases are about three orders of magnitude fewer.
- So the continuous extension is uneconomic **as Euler**. Any continuous-target claim must use a higher-order or validated integrator with its own cost accounting; the discrete Euler certificate stays a discrete-recurrence claim only.
