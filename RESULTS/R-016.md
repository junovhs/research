## Task, alternatives and decision

For D=16 periodic sites solve the *semidiscrete* Allen–Cahn ODE with positive heterogeneous initial components in [0.5,2], and query u_0(11.5) at absolute tolerance 1e-9. It is a selected continuous-time ODE component, **not** the spatial-continuum PDE. An unforced prefix lasts 10.5 units; one unit of known, possibly spatially varying forcing follows. Does R-015's cheap phase-aware scalar-extrema enclosure still admit? Compare it with spatially resolved extremal evolution and a sampled full initial-value solve. A strong alternative is a validated adaptive high-order full-state specialist with a goal-oriented error bound and access to the same analytic-prefix shortcut; it was **not implemented or timed here**. Certified reuse across changed nonlinear queries is another *untested* competing research direction. This cheap experiment tests a potential failure before investing in a solver.

## Mathematical construction and boundary

The model is u'_i=(u_(i-1)-2u_i+u_(i+1)) + f(u_i)+b_i(t), f(u)=u-u^3, with periodic indices and piecewise constant b_i=0 for t in [0,10.5]. The explicit 16-element heterogeneous initial field and all forcing case names are in the archived JSON. Tail cases: zero pulse; uniform -0.25; cosine 0.25 cos(2 pi i/16); localized +0.25 only at site 0.

Cooperative comparison and the exact positive scalar unforced flow F_t(x)=1/sqrt(1+(x^-2-1)exp(-2t)) imply every coordinate at t_0=10.5 belongs to [L0,U0]=[F_t0(0.5),F_t0(2)]. Two exact-ODE comparison algorithms then bracket all permitted initial fields:

1. Global scalar extrema: scalar l'=f(l)+min_i b_i and h'=f(h)+max_i b_i, from L0,U0. This erases the actual spatial drive pattern.
2. Spatial extremal fields: start two D-site ODEs at constant vectors L0 and U0, with the SAME sitewise forcing b_i(t). Comparison brackets the queried coordinate without replaying the unforced prefix.

For the cosine case, the first method uses constant tail forcing -1/4 and +1/4. Both scalars remain inside [4/5,6/5]: f(4/5)-1/4=0.038>0, f(6/5)+1/4=-0.278<0. Since f'(x)=1-3x^2 is at least -83/25 in this box, the width w=h-l satisfies w' >= -(83/25)w+1/2 with w(0)>=0. Therefore

    w(1) >= (25/166)*(1-exp(-83/25)) = approximately 0.145157706.

This is a **lower bound on this global scalar-extrema algorithm's returned uncertainty**, not a universal obstruction to phase-aware spatial methods. No numerical accuracy improvement to scalar endpoints can make its width <=1e-9 here. The spatial-extremal comparison is a mathematical exact-flow inclusion *conditional on exact time evolution*, not a floating-point certified method.
