# R-021 · For the continuous Allen–Cahn target, the fair baseline is adaptive high-order integration, not Euler: about 10³ evaluations at D=16 and 1.3×10⁷ scalar updates at R-014 scale

**Date:** 2026-10-14 UTC. **Track: EXPLORATORY: YES; PREREGISTERED: NO; POD: NO; NOVELTY UNVERIFIED. Evidence: REPORTED**, two source-bound COMPLETE archives (R-021 and R-022 runs). **Claim class: KNOWN_METHOD_BENCHMARK**: the integrators are standard, and the contribution is a measured work-to-accuracy comparison for this family, with the R-020 comparator corrected.

## Decision gate

**Question.** R-020 concluded that a 1e-9 continuous-model certificate is uneconomic because explicit Euler needs about 10⁹ steps. Is that a valid economic conclusion, or is Euler the wrong comparator? Two sub-questions: (a) for the D=16 setup of R-020, how many evaluations do standard higher-order integrators need to reach 1e-9? (b) at R-014 scale (D=512, horizon ≈258.7), what does a full-domain adaptive integrator cost?

**Target and accuracy layer.** Terminal state in max-norm, against a DOP853 reference. Accuracy layer: **time discretization only**. These are *non-rigorous* empirical costs, not certificates. No spatial or model error is included.

**Equations** (both runs): periodic cubic Allen–Cahn on D sites, u_i' = d(u_{i+1} − 2u_i + u_{i−1}) + u_i − u_i³ + A·s(t)·cos(2πi/D), d=1, A=0.5. R-021: D=16, T=8, s piecewise linear through the R-020 knots. R-022: D=512, T=258.703125, s piecewise linear through 33 knots with seeded uniform values in [−1,1] (endpoints 0), random initial state in [−0.9,0.9] (seed 20261014). **R-022's forcing and initial data are its own construction, not R-014's exact generator.**

**Strongest alternatives.** Adaptive high-order explicit RK (DOP853), fixed-step RK4 (the cheapest high-order comparator), and Euler. Rigorous validated integrators (van den Berg–Breden 2026, arXiv 2601.05146: spectral/Chebyshev fixed-point with Intlab interval arithmetic) were *not* run, since they need Matlab/Intlab and are not implementable here; they are the rigorous rival and their cost is unmeasured.

**Decision rule fixed before the run.** A continuous omission claim is worth pursuing only if its total certified cost beats the best non-rigorous adaptive full-domain cost at the same accuracy, *and* it still carries a validated continuous bound. Measured here: the first part sets the bar.

Reference self-consistency: rtol 1e-13 vs 1e-12 differ by **4.6e-14**.

Reading: RK4 reaches 1e-9 at about 4096 evaluations; DOP853 reaches 1e-10 in about 824 evaluations and is ≈30 ms wall time. Euler (R-020) would need about 10⁹ steps, so **Euler is not a credible comparator for this target**.

Reference: rtol=1e-12, 25,720 evaluations. **This reference was not cross-checked at a second tolerance**; the (a) cross-check suggests the reference error is well below 1e-9 but that is not verified here.

R-014's discrete Euler full replay at its own scale (N=16557, D=512) is 8.5×10⁶ updates, but it targets the *exact discrete* recurrence, not the continuous ODE; the two are not interchangeable baselines.

## Checks and limits

- (a) reference is cross-checked (4.6e-14 gap). (b) is not.
- Forcing and initial data in (b) are a stand-in, not R-014's generator. Results transfer as a scaling statement only.
- Non-rigorous: DOP853 tolerances are not certificates; the observed errors are against a reference, not an enclosure.
- The rigorous rival (validated spectral/Chebyshev integrator, arXiv 2601.05146) was **not** measured; its cost is the missing number for a fair continuous claim.
- Single-agent review. No independent reviewer.

## Consequence (correction to R-020)

R-020's conclusion that the continuous extension is "uneconomic" rested on explicit Euler as comparator. That comparison is invalid: a standard adaptive integrator needs about 10³ evaluations at D=16, not 10⁹ steps. **Correction:** [RESULTS/R-020-correction.md](R-020-correction.md). R-020's measured first-order Euler constant is unchanged.

## Decision

**PARK the continuous omission claim.** Continuous certification must be benchmarked against two baselines: this adaptive full-domain cost (≈1.3×10⁷ updates at R-014 scale, non-rigorous) and the rigorous validated integrator family, whose cost is unmeasured. The R-014 discrete result is unchanged. Next measurable step, if pursued: run a validated spectral integrator on the D=16 problem and record its cost to a certified 1e-9 bound. That is a larger implementation than this session can justify without a concrete workload.

**Opportunity cost.** The discrete omission line already has a demonstrated mechanism but no workload (R-019 gate); the continuous line now has a clear, measured bar that a rigorous integrator must clear. This is a more useful decision than the R-020 Euler park.
